**A standby generator is judged on one moment: when the power fails, does it start and carry the load?** Most of its life is spent waiting, so the only way to know is to run it. Exercising and load bank testing are how that gets proven between outages, and the log is how the customer can show it was done.

This page describes generator exercising and load testing at the category level. How often, for how long, and at what load all come from the generator manufacturer's documentation and any requirements that apply to the installation.

## Exercising versus load bank testing

- **Exercising** runs the generator on a schedule so its engine, fuel system, and controls do not sit idle indefinitely.
- **Load bank testing** connects the generator to a controlled artificial load so it can be run at a meaningful level without depending on the building's load. A diesel that only ever runs lightly loaded can develop problems that a proper load run reveals.

## Why the record matters

For many generator customers, such as facilities, property managers, rental fleets, and anyone with critical equipment, the record is not paperwork. It is how they show their team, their insurer, or an inspector that the generator was maintained and tested. A generator log that shows what was run and what was found is part of what the customer is paying for.

Some installations have their own testing requirements, set by codes or standards that apply to that site. Those requirements belong to the installation, not to a generic checklist. Confirm them with the customer.

## What the customer's record should show

After each visit the record should show what exercise or load test was run, how the generator performed, what was serviced, and what is due next. See [what belongs in a PM report for the customer](/kb/what-belongs-in-a-pm-report-for-the-customer).
