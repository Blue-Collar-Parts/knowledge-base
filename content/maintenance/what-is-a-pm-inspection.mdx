A preventive maintenance inspection (PMI), often shortened to PM inspection, is a structured way to record a scheduled equipment visit. It turns an otherwise forgettable service stop into a record: what equipment was seen, what condition was observed, what work was completed, and what context the next technician needs.

The useful word is **record**. A blank checklist is only a starting point. The value comes from completing it against a specific asset and keeping the result available when the equipment returns.

## What belongs in a PM record

For most equipment, a PM record needs four blocks of information:

1. **Identify the asset.** Use the unit, asset, VIN, serial number, or other identifier that lets the customer distinguish this machine from the rest of the fleet.
2. **Place the visit in context.** Record the date, technician, and relevant odometer, hour-meter, or operating context.
3. **Document the observation and work.** Organize the visit by system so a reader can see what was checked, what was found, and what was repaired, adjusted, or deferred.
4. **Make the next step clear.** Note follow-up work, a recommendation, or the information needed to plan the next service according to the manufacturer’s guidance.

This structure works because it answers the questions a customer asks after the truck, generator, or vessel is no longer in the bay: *What happened? What needs attention? When should we look at it again?*

## A PM form is not a service manual

The manufacturer’s documentation remains the authority for equipment-specific procedures, intervals, limits, fluids, and parts. A PM record should preserve what the technician did under that guidance; it should not pretend that a generic online form defines the correct maintenance program for every make, model, or operating condition.

## A PM inspection is not a DOT annual inspection

Preventive maintenance and a federal annual DOT inspection can happen around the same equipment, but they do different jobs. A PM visit helps maintain and document equipment. It does not certify a vehicle under 49 CFR Part 396, determine whether a vehicle is out of service, or prove compliance. Read [PM inspection vs. DOT inspection](/kb/pm-inspection-vs-dot-inspection) for the boundary.
