A diesel generator preventive maintenance (PM) checklist organizes the service by system, so nothing on the unit is skipped and the visit can be recorded in a consistent order. The specific items and service intervals come from the engine and generator manufacturer's documentation, not from a generic checklist. ## What a diesel generator PM covers

A PM for a diesel generator set is usually grouped into seven areas:

1. **Engine oil and filter.** Oil condition is one of the clearest signs of how the engine is being run and maintained.
2. **Fuel system and day tank.** Fuel can sit for long stretches in a standby unit. Fuel quality, contamination, and the condition of the tank all affect whether the engine starts and keeps running.
3. **Engine coolant and cooling system.** Coolant protects the engine against corrosion and, in outdoor installations, against freezing. The cooling system is checked so the unit can carry load without overheating.
4. **Air intake and exhaust system.** The engine needs clean air in and a sound exhaust path out. A restricted intake or a damaged exhaust hurts performance and is easy to miss between visits.
5. **Battery and charging system.** A generator that cannot crank is not a backup. Battery condition and charging output decide whether the unit starts when called on.
6. **Load-bank exercise record and operating maintenance.** Backup units may go long periods without real load. This section keeps a record of exercise and testing so the history of how the unit has been run is on file.
7. **Electrical system and controls.** Automatic start and stop controls, emergency shutdown systems, and the interface to the rest of the installation all have to work together when the unit is needed.

Which checks fall under each area, how often they are done, and what is acceptable all depend on the manufacturer's documentation for that particular unit.

## Why keep a generator maintenance log

Standby generators run rarely, so a unit can go months with little change on the hour meter. That makes the written record more important than memory. A generator maintenance log is a dated history of each service: what was looked at, what was done, and what was recommended next. It is how the next technician knows the unit's condition without starting from scratch, and how the customer can see what has been done on equipment they may not think about until it is needed.
