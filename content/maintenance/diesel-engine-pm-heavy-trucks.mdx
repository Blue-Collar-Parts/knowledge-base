**The engine side of a heavy-truck PM centers on the oil and filter change, plus whatever an oil sample or a scheduled overhead (valve) adjustment adds to that visit.** Capacities and intervals are not one number for every truck. They come from the engine manufacturer's documentation for that engine and the way the truck is run. This page covers the heavy-truck engine. For oil, transmission and hydraulic fluids across trucks, generators and marine engines, see [engine oil, transmission, and hydraulic fluids](/kb/diesel-engine-oil-and-fluids-maintenance).

## What does a semi truck oil change cover?

An oil change on a heavy-truck diesel is more than a drain and refill. The technician:

- replaces the oil filter, and any additional or bypass filter the engine is built with;
- fills to the quantity and oil specification the engine manufacturer gives for that engine and its oil pan, then checks the level;
- looks at what came out: metal in the filter, a fuel smell, or milky oil each point somewhere specific;
- takes an oil sample, when the customer or the maintenance plan calls for one.

The capacity depends on the engine, and on some engines on the oil pan and the number of filters fitted. The engine pages below give each manufacturer's published figure with its source.

## What does a diesel oil analysis tell you?

A lab report on an oil sample reads the engine in ways a visual check cannot:

- **Wear metals,** such as iron, copper, aluminum and lead, show which parts are shedding material.
- **Contaminants** point to a failure path. Coolant in the oil points toward a leak from the cooling system, fuel points toward the fuel system, and silicon (dirt) points toward the air intake.
- **Oil condition,** such as viscosity and oxidation, shows whether the oil is still doing its job.

One sample is a snapshot. The value is in the trend across visits on the same engine, so the result belongs in that engine's service record, next to the rest of the visit, where the next technician will see it.

## Where does the maintenance schedule come from?

"What is the maintenance schedule for a semi truck?" has no single answer. Engine manufacturers publish service intervals by duty cycle, so the same engine can be due at different mileages depending on how the truck is run. Cummins, for example, sets the X15 oil drain interval by fuel economy band, and Detroit sets the DD15's by duty category. The engine pages quote those intervals where the manufacturer's document gives them. Blue Collar Parts does not set intervals of its own.
