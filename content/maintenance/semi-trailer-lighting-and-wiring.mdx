A trailer's lights and its ABS module both depend on the same connection between tractor and trailer, so trailer light repair and trailer wiring problems often start in the same place: the connector, the harness, and the grounds.

## The 7-way connector

The standard connector between a tractor and semi trailer carries ground, running lights, turn signals, stop lights, and — on most trailers — a powered auxiliary circuit for the ABS module. The pin assignment for that connector is standardized in SAE J560; this page doesn't reproduce the pin chart. Use the standard or the vehicle's wiring documentation for the assignment on the unit in front of you. A connector with bent, corroded or missing pins, or a worn locking mechanism that lets it work loose on the road, is one of the most common sources of intermittent lighting and ABS faults.

## Grounds

A trailer's lighting circuit depends on a good ground path back to the tractor, usually carried through the connector itself and through the trailer's own frame and fixture mounting. A poor ground shows up as dim, flickering, or cross-connected lights — a turn signal that also lights the brake lamp, for example — more often than as a light that simply doesn't work at all. Check ground connections at the connector, at the harness splices, and at each light fixture's mount before replacing a bulb or lamp that tests fine.

## The harness

The wiring harness runs the length of the trailer, exposed to road spray, salt, flexing at the coupling, and contact with cargo and the frame. Chafed insulation, a pinched or kinked run, and corrosion inside a connector or splice are the common failure points. A harness repair should be made with connectors and sealing rated for the exposure a trailer harness sees, and routed and secured the way the original run was, away from moving parts and sources of heat or abrasion.

## Lights and fixtures

Most trailer marker, clearance, turn and brake lights in service today are sealed LED units rather than lamps with replaceable bulbs. A sealed light that's dim, flickering, or dark is usually a wiring or ground problem upstream of the fixture, or the fixture itself failed and needs replacement — not something to be opened or repaired. Confirm the fixture's own connector and seal are intact before condemning the light as failed.

## Working through a lighting or ABS wiring complaint

Because the 7-way connector, the grounds and the harness are shared by lighting and the ABS module, a wiring-based lighting problem and an ABS fault can show up from the same root cause. See [trailer ABS troubleshooting](/kb/trailer-abs-module) for how to work the ABS side of that same connection.

## What should the service record show?

A record of trailer lighting and wiring repair should show:

- which lights were out, dim or cross-connected, and on which circuit;
- the cause found: connector pins, a ground, a harness run, or the fixture;
- what was repaired or replaced, with part numbers and where on the trailer;
- a full light check after the repair.

A record kept by the trailer's own unit number or VIN stays with the trailer, whichever tractor pulled it.
