**A semi truck PM checklist goes system by system: engine oil, fuel, coolant, air intake, belts, transmission and driveline, battery and charging, steering and suspension, tires, wheels, brakes, lights, and the fifth wheel.** For each system, the technician looks at condition, leaks, wear and function, and writes down what they found and what they did. The items below are what to look at. Specifications, limits and service intervals come from the manufacturer's documentation for that truck.

## Engine and drivetrain checklist

**Engine oil and filter.** See [diesel engine PM for heavy trucks](/kb/diesel-engine-pm-heavy-trucks).

- Oil level on the dipstick or sight glass
- Oil color and clarity, and any fuel smell or milky look
- Oil filter condition, and any bypass filter
- Drain plug and threads, and any leak around the oil pan

**Fuel system.** See [diesel fuel system maintenance](/kb/diesel-fuel-system-maintenance).

- Fuel filters, primary and secondary where fitted
- Water separator: drain it and note any free water
- Fuel tanks: leaks, corrosion and mounting
- Fuel lines and return line: leaks, kinks and cracks
- Fuel cap present and sealing

**Cooling system.** See [diesel engine cooling system maintenance](/kb/diesel-engine-cooling-system-maintenance).

- Coolant level, checked cold, at the recovery tank or radiator
- Coolant color and concentration, with a refractometer or test strips
- Coolant filter condition, where fitted
- Coolant hoses and clamps: leaks, cracks and loose clamps

**Air intake and exhaust.** See [air intake and exhaust maintenance](/kb/diesel-air-intake-and-exhaust-maintenance).

- Air filter element and the restriction indicator
- Air filter housing seal, with no path for air to bypass the element
- Intake ducts and clamps: cracks, loose clamps and debris
- Exhaust pipes, clamps and mounts: leaks and heat damage

**Belts and pulleys.**

- Drive belts: cracks, fraying, glazing and slipping
- Idler and tensioner pulleys: noise, bearing play and mounting
- Belt tension, where the drive is adjustable

**Transmission and driveline.** See [engine oil, transmission and hydraulic fluids](/kb/diesel-engine-oil-and-fluids-maintenance).

- Transmission fluid level, and its color and smell
- Differential gear oil level at the fill plug
- Transmission pan, seals and drain plug: leaks
- Driveshaft, U-joints and carrier bearing: play and damage

**Battery and charging.** See [battery and charging system maintenance](/kb/diesel-battery-and-charging-system-maintenance).

- Battery voltage, and a load test where one is done
- Battery terminals and cables: corrosion and tight connections
- Battery hold-downs and box
- Alternator output

## Chassis checklist

**Steering and suspension.**

- Power steering reservoir level and fluid condition
- Power steering hoses and fittings: leaks, chafing and routing
- Steering linkage, tie rod ends and drag link: play and damage
- Springs, hangers and U-bolts: cracks, shifted leaves and loose fasteners
- Air springs, where fitted: leaks, chafing and damage
- Shock absorbers: leaks and mounting

**Tires.** See [semi truck and trailer tires](/kb/semi-truck-and-trailer-tires), [tread depth and condition](/kb/truck-tire-tread-depth-and-condition) and [tire pressure and inflation](/kb/semi-truck-tire-pressure-and-inflation).

- Tread depth on the steer tires and on the drive tires
- Inflation, and any flat tire or audible leak
- Tread and sidewall condition: cuts and damage
- Dual spacing and matching

**Wheels and hubs.** See [wheel seal leaks and replacement](/kb/wheel-seal-leaks-and-replacement).

- Lug nuts and studs: condition and fit, and rust trails
- Wheel and rim condition, including the stud holes
- Hub oil level where oil-filled, and wheel seals for leaks

**Brakes and air system.** See [semi truck brake inspection](/kb/semi-truck-brake-inspection) and [how an air brake system works](/kb/air-brake-system).

- Brake chambers and slack adjusters: leaks, damage and missing parts
- Pushrod travel at each wheel, measured and recorded
- Brake linings or pads, and any oil or grease on them
- Drums and rotors: cracks and heat checking
- Air hoses and tubing: chafing, cracks and crimps
- Air tanks drained, noting any water or oil
- Low-air warning operates
- Tractor protection valve and glad hands

**Lights and reflectors.** See [semi trailer lighting and wiring](/kb/semi-trailer-lighting-and-wiring).

- Headlamps, turn signals and hazard lamps operate
- Stop and tail lamps operate
- Clearance and marker lamps, and reflectors
- Trailer cord and plug: damage and corrosion

**Fifth wheel and coupling.**

- Fifth wheel plate: cracks, wear and lubrication
- Locking jaws and release handle operate
- Mounting bolts and brackets: condition and fit
- Slider locking pins engage, where fitted

Each system is a place to record what was looked at, what was found, what was done and what needs follow-up. The manufacturer's documentation still decides how to check each item and what counts as worn out.

## How often should a semi truck get a PM?

There is no single PM interval for every semi truck. Engine manufacturers publish their own service intervals and set them by how the truck is run, using miles, hours, fuel economy, idle time or a duty category. The same engine can be due at different points on two trucks.

The engine pages give the manufacturer's published oil change intervals, each quoted from a named document: [Cummins X15](/kb/cummins-x15-oil-capacity), [Detroit DD13 and DD15](/kb/detroit-dd13-dd15-oil-capacity) and [PACCAR MX-13](/kb/paccar-mx13-oil-capacity). Confirm the interval against the documentation for the specific engine serial number and build. Blue Collar Parts does not set PM intervals of its own.

## A PM is not a DOT annual inspection

A PM visit maintains and documents equipment. It is not a federal annual inspection, it does not decide whether a vehicle is out of service, and it is not evidence of compliance. Read [PM inspection vs. DOT inspection](/kb/pm-inspection-vs-dot-inspection) for the boundary.
