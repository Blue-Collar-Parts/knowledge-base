A semi truck preventive maintenance (PM) checklist, as described in [What Is a Preventive Maintenance Inspection?](/kb/what-is-a-pm-inspection), organizes the service by system. It gives the technician a clear order of work and gives the reader a clear record of what was looked at.

A checklist does not decide what a given truck needs. The specific items and intervals come from the manufacturer's service documentation for that make, model, and duty cycle. Use this page to see how a PM is grouped, not to set a maintenance program.

## What a semi truck PM covers

A PM on an over-the-road tractor is usually grouped into these eight systems.

1. **Engine oil and filter.** Oil is the first thing to show how an engine is being run and maintained. This section records the condition of the oil and filter and the oil service performed.
2. **Fuel system.** Dirty or contaminated fuel hurts the injection system and the engine behind it. This section covers fuel cleanliness, water, filtration, and the tanks.
3. **Engine coolant and coolant filter.** Coolant protects the engine from heat and corrosion, and it wears out over time. This section records coolant condition and how the cooling system is being treated.
4. **Air intake and air filtration.** An engine needs clean air, and a restricted intake costs power and fuel. This section covers the filter and the path air takes to the engine.
5. **Belts and hoses (drive system).** Belts turn the accessories and hoses carry coolant and fluids. A failure in either can stop a truck, so this section records their condition.
6. **Transmission and driveline fluid.** Gear oil keeps the transmission and driveline lubricated. This section covers fluid condition and level, and any sign of a leak.
7. **Hydraulic system (if equipped).** Power steering and auxiliary hydraulics depend on clean fluid and sound lines. This section applies only when the truck has the system.
8. **Electrical and battery.** Starting and charging problems are a common reason a truck does not run. This section records battery condition, the charging system, and the connections.

Each system is a place to record what the technician saw, what was done, and what needs follow-up. The manufacturer's documentation still decides what to check in each one and when.

## A PM is not a DOT annual inspection

A PM visit maintains and documents equipment. It is not a federal annual inspection, it does not decide whether a vehicle is out of service, and it is not evidence of compliance. Read [PM inspection vs. DOT inspection](/kb/pm-inspection-vs-dot-inspection) for the boundary.

## What your customer should receive

The customer should get a record they can read without calling the technician: the truck, the date, what was found by system, what was done, and what comes next. [See what belongs in a PM report for the customer](/kb/what-belongs-in-a-pm-report-for-the-customer).
