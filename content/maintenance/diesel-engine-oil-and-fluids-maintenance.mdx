**Fluids are where most PM visits start, and for good reason: they are the cheapest part of the equipment to service and the most expensive to neglect.** Oil and fluid condition also tells a trained technician a great deal about what is happening inside components nobody can see without taking them apart.

This page describes the fluid side of a PM at the category level. Fluid specifications, capacities, and change intervals come from the manufacturer's documentation for that engine, transmission, or system.

## Engine oil and filter

Engine oil lubricates, cools, cleans, and seals, and the filter keeps what the oil picks up from going back around. Every diesel PM form gives the engine oil and filter its own section, whether the engine is in a tractor, a generator, or a boat. Recording what the oil looked like and what was serviced gives the next technician a baseline to compare against.

## Transmission, driveline, and marine gear

The components that carry the engine's power to the wheels or the propeller have their own fluids and seals: the transmission and differentials on a truck, the gearbox on a marine engine. They get their own line in the record, separate from the engine, because a problem there is a different repair with a different cost.

## Hydraulic systems, where equipped

Trucks with hydraulic equipment carry another fluid system. When it is present, the PM records its condition alongside the rest.

## Why the record matters more here than anywhere

Fluid service repeats on a schedule, and a change in condition between visits is often the first sign of a problem. A record that shows what was found at each visit lets the customer, and the next technician, see the trend.
