**There is no single "diesel mechanic license" in the United States.** The credential most heavy-duty technicians mean when they say "certified" is ASE certification: a set of tests from the National Institute for Automotive Service Excellence, plus documented work experience. Performing federal annual DOT inspections is a separate question, answered by a federal qualification rather than a certificate. This page sorts out which is which.

## The ASE Medium-Heavy Truck tests (T-series)

ASE certifies medium- and heavy-duty truck technicians through the T-series tests:

| Test | Subject |
|---|---|
| T1 | Gasoline Engines |
| T2 | Diesel Engines |
| T3 | Drive Train |
| T4 | Brakes |
| T5 | Suspension and Steering |
| T6 | Electrical/Electronic Systems |
| T7 | Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning (HVAC) |
| T8 | Preventive Maintenance Inspection |

Passing a test is half of it. ASE also requires full-time, hands-on work experience (or a combination of formal training and experience), and the amount can vary by test. A passed test is not certified until ASE has reviewed and approved your work experience. Check the current requirement for each test on ase.com before you register.

**Master Medium-Heavy Truck Technician.** Passing T2 through T8 earns Master status. T1 (gasoline engines) is not required for it.

**Five-year cycle.** ASE credentials are valid for five years. Recertification tests (T1R to T8R) are shorter than the initial tests. Recertifying early does not carry over the time left on the old credential; the new five years start on the recertification date.

## The T8 Preventive Maintenance Inspection test

T8 is the ASE test closest to the daily work of a technician who does PM service. Its task list in ASE's official study guide works through a whole-truck inspection, for example:

- Cab instruments and gauges, controls, wipers and washers, and HVAC controls
- The paperwork and electronics on the truck: permits, registration, decals, inspection papers, and ELD connections
- Batteries, starting, and charging
- Frame, body-attaching hardware, and equipment such as lift gates and rear impact guards
- Tires, wheels, and the rest of the chassis

Read the official ASE Medium/Heavy Truck study guide for the current task list and test specifications. Question counts and times change between editions.

T8 measures whether you know how to perform a thorough PM inspection. It does not make a PM inspection into a regulatory inspection, and it is not a DOT inspector certificate. See [PM inspection vs. DOT inspection](/kb/pm-inspection-vs-dot-inspection).

## How do I get certified to do DOT inspections?

No federal agency issues a "DOT inspector certification." The federal rule for who may perform an annual inspection sets qualifications instead: understanding the inspection criteria, mastering the methods and tools, and either a qualifying training program or certificate, or at least a year of qualifying training or experience. Brake work has its own qualification.

ASE certification is not named in that rule. An ASE training path may help show the training-or-experience prong, but the rule is written around what you completed and how long you have done the work. Read [how to become a DOT inspector](/kb/who-can-perform-dot-inspection) for the full qualification.

## Other credentials you will see

- **A commercial driver's license (CDL).** Required to drive many commercial vehicles. Passing the CDL air brake tests matters for some brake inspection work.
- **Manufacturer training.** Engine, transmission, and equipment makers run their own training and certificates for their products.
- **State programs.** Some States run inspection programs with their own inspector requirements.
- **Refrigerant handling.** Technicians who service vehicle air conditioning commonly need certification for refrigerant handling. Confirm the current requirement for the work you do.

## What the credential does not do

A credential says what you were tested on. The customer still judges the work by what you found, what you fixed, and how clearly you showed it. A clear [complaint, cause, and correction](/kb/complaint-cause-correction) on every job is the part of the record a certificate cannot write for you.
