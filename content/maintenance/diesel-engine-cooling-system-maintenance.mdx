**A diesel engine turns a large share of its fuel into heat, and the cooling system's job is to carry that heat away at a steady rate.** When it falls behind, the engine protects itself, loses power, or fails. That is why every diesel PM, whether on a highway tractor, a standby generator, or a boat, gives cooling its own section of the record.

This page describes the cooling system at the category level. What to check, how to test it, and how often all come from the engine manufacturer's documentation.

## What the cooling system has to do

The coolant loop moves heat from the engine block to a place it can be released: a radiator on a truck or a generator, a heat exchanger on most marine engines. The coolant also carries the chemistry that protects the inside of the engine. A PM looks at both jobs: is the system moving heat, and is the coolant still protecting the metal it touches?

## How it differs by equipment

- **Trucks and tractors** run long hours under changing load, airflow, and weather. Cooling problems often show up first as a change the driver reports, which makes the complaint line of the write-up worth reading closely.
- **Diesel generators** may sit idle for weeks and then have to carry full load immediately. A standby unit's cooling system has to be ready on demand, often with a heater keeping the engine warm while it waits.
- **Marine diesels** usually have two circuits: the engine's closed coolant loop, and a raw-water circuit that draws water from outside the hull to cool it. Raw water brings debris, growth, and corrosion with it, so marine PM pays separate attention to that circuit and the parts that move water through it.

## Belts and hoses belong with cooling

Belts drive the pumps that move coolant and raw water, and hoses carry it. Both age quietly between visits. Recording their condition at each PM is how wear gets planned for instead of discovered at the roadside or on the water.

## What the customer's record should show

A useful cooling section tells the customer what was looked at, what condition it was in, what was serviced or replaced, and anything to plan for at the next visit. Where a test or reading informed the result, keep it with the finding. See [what belongs in a PM report for the customer](/kb/what-belongs-in-a-pm-report-for-the-customer).
