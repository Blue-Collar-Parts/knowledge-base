**A diesel engine is only as healthy as the fuel that reaches its injectors.** Modern fuel systems run at very high pressure with very tight clearances, and dirt or water that gets past filtration can do expensive damage. A PM treats the fuel path from the tank through filtration as its own section of the record.

This page describes the fuel system at the category level. Filter types, service intervals, and procedures come from the engine manufacturer's documentation.

## Water is the problem worth naming

Water gets into diesel fuel through condensation, contaminated deliveries, and, on boats, the environment itself. Fuel-water separators and filters are there to stop it, and a PM confirms they are doing their job. Microbial growth at the boundary between water and fuel is a related problem in fuel that sits for long periods.

## How it differs by equipment

- **Trucks and tractors** burn fuel quickly, so fuel rarely sits long. Filtration and water separation still decide how well the engine runs between PMs.
- **Diesel generators** often store fuel for months in a base tank or a separate day tank. Stored fuel ages, and a standby unit that cannot get clean fuel when the power fails has failed at the one moment that matters. Generator PM looks at the tank as part of the system.
- **Marine diesels** live with moisture, and the whole fuel path from the tank onward is exposed to it. Water in the fuel on the water is a safety problem, not only a maintenance one.

## What the customer's record should show

The fuel section should tell the customer what was inspected, what was drained or replaced, what condition the fuel and filtration were found in, and what to plan for next. If you found water or contamination, say where and what you did about it. See [what belongs in a PM report for the customer](/kb/what-belongs-in-a-pm-report-for-the-customer).
