**There is no single mandatory federal form for the annual DOT inspection.** 49 CFR 396.21 does not prescribe a layout. It specifies what the report must contain, and any form that records those six things, accurately and completely, can serve as the report. The qualified inspector who performs the inspection prepares it.

## What must the annual inspection report contain?

Under §396.21(a), the report must:

1. **Identify the individual performing the inspection.**
2. **Identify the motor carrier operating the vehicle**, or the intermodal equipment provider intending to interchange it to a motor carrier.
3. **Identify the date of the inspection.**
4. **Identify the vehicle inspected.**
5. **Identify the vehicle components inspected and describe the results**, including the identification of those components not meeting the minimum standards in [Appendix A to Part 396](/kb/appendix-a-inspection-standards).
6. **Certify the accuracy and completeness of the inspection** as complying with all the requirements of the section.

Item 5 is the one a thin form gets wrong. The rule asks for the components inspected and a description of the results, with the components that did not meet Appendix A identified. A single "pass" checkbox and a signature records a verdict, not the inspection.

## Does each vehicle in a combination need its own report?

Each vehicle has to be inspected. §396.17(a) says the term commercial motor vehicle includes each vehicle in a combination: for a tractor, semitrailer, and full trailer, the tractor, the semitrailer, and the full trailer (including the converter dolly, if so equipped) must each be inspected. Because the report identifies "the vehicle inspected," a form should make clear which unit each set of results belongs to.

## Who keeps the report, and for how long?

- **Fourteen months.** The original or a copy is retained by the motor carrier, intermodal equipment provider, or other entity responsible for the inspection, for fourteen months from the date of the report (§396.21(b)(1)).
- **Where the vehicle is housed or maintained.** That is where the original or copy must be kept (§396.21(b)(1)).
- **On demand.** It must be available for inspection on demand of an authorized Federal, State, or local official (§396.21(b)(2)).

Fourteen months is longer than the inspection is good for. The inspection covers 12 months under §396.17(c); the report has to last two months beyond that. See [recordkeeping requirements](/kb/dot-inspection-recordkeeping) for the other retention clocks in Part 396.

## What if someone else performed the inspection?

The carrier still has to produce it. Under §396.21(b)(3), if the motor carrier operating the vehicle did not perform the last annual inspection, the carrier is responsible for obtaining the original or a copy of that report on demand of an authorized official. The same applies to an intermodal equipment provider that did not perform the inspection itself.

For a service provider inspecting a customer's equipment, this is why the customer needs a usable copy of the report, not only a decal on the unit.

## Is the decal the same as the report?

No. The decal is documentation **based on** the report, and §396.17(c)(2) allows it in place of carrying the report on the vehicle. The report still has to exist and still has to be retained for fourteen months. See [what the inspection decal must show](/kb/dot-inspection-decal-requirements).

## Is a driver vehicle inspection report (DVIR) the same thing?

No. The driver vehicle inspection report under §396.11 is prepared by the driver at the completion of each day's work. The annual inspection report under §396.21 is prepared by a [qualified inspector](/kb/who-can-perform-dot-inspection) and records an inspection against Appendix A. One does not substitute for the other.

## A practical check before the report is signed

Read the form against the six items above:

- Is the inspector named?
- Is the carrier (or intermodal equipment provider) named?
- Is the date there?
- Is the unit identified clearly enough that nobody can confuse it with another?
- Are the inspected components and their results described, with any component that fell short of Appendix A identified?
- Is the certification there?

If any of those is missing, the form is missing part of what §396.21(a) asks for.
