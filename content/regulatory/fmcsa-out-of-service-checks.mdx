**Four checks (tires, brakes, air hoses and air leaks) covered 67.8% of the tractors put out of service at roadside in the last 12 months, and five checks (tires, lamps, brakes, the breakaway system and cargo securement) covered 75.5% of the trailers.** "Covered" means the unit had at least one out-of-service violation in one of those checks. This page lists the checks, in order, for tractors and for trailers.

## How these numbers were made

- **Dataset:** FMCSA vehicle inspection data, public roadside inspections, one current version per inspection.
- **Window:** inspections from 2025-10-19 to 2026-10-18.
- **Unit:** one inspected tractor or trailer in one inspection. A truck inspected twice counts twice.
- **Population:** units with at least one violation carrying the out-of-service flag. Tractors: n = 168,252. Trailers: n = 190,523.
- **What the number is:** a mix among units put out of service. It is not a rate, because units inspected with no violations are not in this data. It says what the units put out of service had, not how likely any truck is to be put out of service.
- **Checks:** each check is a family of FMCSA violation codes that this page groups by code prefix. A unit can have violations in several checks, so the shares add to more than 100%. Units whose only out-of-service violations fall in no listed check count in n but in no check, so coverage does not reach 100%.

## Which checks show up most on tractors put out of service?

Tractors put out of service, n = 168,252. The cumulative column is the share of tractors that had an out-of-service violation in that check or any check above it.

| Rank | Check | Tractors with an out-of-service violation in it | Share of tractors put out of service | Cumulative share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Tires: flat, or under half rated pressure | 71,035 | 42.2% | 42.2% |
| 2 | Brakes: 20% or more defective | 21,802 | 13.0% | 53.7% |
| 3 | Air hoses and tubes damaged | 18,091 | 10.8% | 62.9% |
| 4 | Air leaks and air-loss-rate failures | 10,984 | 6.5% | 67.8% |
| 5 | Cargo securement | 4,995 | 3.0% | 70.2% |
| 6 | Hub and wheel-seal leaks | 4,684 | 2.8% | 72.1% |
| 7 | Lamps and reflectors | 4,313 | 2.6% | 74.2% |
| 8 | Fuel leaks | 3,268 | 1.9% | 75.5% |
| 9 | Trailer breakaway system | 3,163 | 1.9% | 76.9% |

Tires were found on 42.2% of the tractors put out of service. The four checks at the top covered 67.8%. For how the air system fits together, see [the air brake system](/kb/air-brake-system).

## How do tractors and trailers differ?

The order differs by equipment type, so "the top checks" is a different list for each. Tires are first on both. On tractors the next three are brakes, air hoses and air leaks; on trailers the next are lamps, brakes and the breakaway system. The breakaway system is a trailer item and appears at rank 9 on tractors.

## What an out-of-service order is

An out-of-service order is a roadside determination by authorized FMCSA personnel under 49 CFR 396.9(c). The page [Unsafe operation, failed inspections and out-of-service orders](/kb/safe-operation-and-out-of-service) explains what it requires and how it differs from a failed annual inspection. The [common DOT inspection questions](/kb/common-dot-inspection-questions) page answers the related questions.

## What these numbers do not say

They describe what inspectors recorded on the units they put out of service. They are not a safety rating or a finding about any carrier, truck or trailer, and they say nothing about whether any vehicle is compliant. Inspectors choose which trucks to stop, so the units here are not a sample of all trucks on the road.
