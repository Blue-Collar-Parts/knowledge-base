A brake chamber air leak is air escaping from the chamber itself, from the hose or tubing that feeds it, or from a fitting between them. Air brakes run on stored air, so a leak at one wheel end is a problem for the whole system.

## What a brake chamber does

A **service brake chamber** turns air pressure into pushrod movement when the driver applies the brakes. A **spring brake chamber** adds a second section behind it that holds the parking and emergency spring back with air pressure. See the [air brake system](/kb/air-brake-system) for where these sit.

## Where brake chamber air leaks show up

- **The chamber.** Air escaping at the clamp ring, at the diaphragm, through a vent or port, or around the pushrod.
- **Hoses and tubing.** Cracked, chafed, kinked or worn hose, and damaged coiled tubing between tractor and trailer.
- **Fittings and connections.** Loose, worn or damaged ends where a line meets a chamber, valve or coupling.

A leak is often found by listening with the brakes applied and released and then confirming the spot with a leak-detecting solution. The manufacturer's documentation says how to test the specific component and what result is acceptable.

## What the rules say

**49 CFR 393.45** covers brake tubing and hoses, hose assemblies and end fittings. In summary:

- brake tubing, hoses, hose assemblies and end fittings must meet the applicable requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard No. 106;
- brake tubing and hose must be long and flexible enough to accommodate normal motion without damage, secured against chafing, kinking or other mechanical damage, and installed so that it does not contact the exhaust system or another source of high temperature;
- connections for air, vacuum or hydraulic braking systems must be installed so the attachment is free of leaks, constrictions or other conditions that would adversely affect the performance of the brake system.

**49 CFR 396.3(a)(1)** says parts and accessories must be in safe and proper operating condition at all times, and it names the parts specified in Part 393 and any additional parts that may affect safety of operation. The duty in 396.3(a) sits with the motor carrier or intermodal equipment provider that controls the vehicle.

Read the full text on eCFR: [49 CFR 393.45](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-393.45) and [49 CFR 396.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/section-396.3). This page does not give a leak limit or test value.

## Brake chamber replacement

Brake chamber replacement is work done from the manufacturer's documentation for that chamber and axle. A few points hold in general:

- **A spring brake chamber stores a great deal of energy.** The power spring is held compressed. Do not disassemble or cut open a spring brake chamber, and do not loosen its parts while the spring is loaded. The manufacturer's documentation says how to cage or release it safely.
- **Replace with the chamber the axle calls for,** in the type and size the manufacturer specifies for that brake.
- **Check the hose and fittings while the chamber is off.** A chamber that failed from a damaged line or fitting will fail again if the line stays.
- **Check adjustment afterward.** Replacing a chamber disturbs the brake's adjustment, so check it again. See [slack adjuster adjustment](/kb/slack-adjuster-adjustment).
- **Secure the vehicle first.** It must not roll while the brakes are apart.
