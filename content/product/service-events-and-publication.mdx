A preventive-maintenance visit is easier to stand behind when it has a durable service record. Blue Collar Parts uses a **service event** to keep the customer, asset, work performed, and future context together.

## What a service event is

A service event is a dated record of work performed on a specific piece of equipment. It records who did the work, what asset it concerned, and the service information the technician entered. A PM service event is private to its owner while it is being created and completed.

Creating and saving a service event record is **free**. There is no charge to keep your own records.

## Intervals and next service

Equipment is serviced on a schedule, but the correct schedule depends on the equipment, its operating conditions, and the manufacturer's documentation. A service record can preserve the date and meter or odometer context that makes the next visit easier to plan; it does not invent an interval or replace equipment-specific guidance.

## A record versus a customer publication

The record a technician creates and the copy an equipment owner can open are distinct things. The current workflow is for recording PM work. Customer-facing publication and distribution are planned capabilities, not promises that a completed record is automatically sent or publicly visible.

That distinction matters commercially: a completed service record gives the technician context; a customer copy should give the equipment owner a concise account of what was checked, found, done, and due next. [See what belongs in that report](/kb/what-belongs-in-a-pm-report-for-the-equipment-owner).

## Who can see what

Service event records are owner-scoped. A technician sees their own customers, assets, and events; a request for another owner’s record does not return a redacted version of it, it returns nothing. Publication, when available, will be an explicit act rather than a side effect of entering work.
