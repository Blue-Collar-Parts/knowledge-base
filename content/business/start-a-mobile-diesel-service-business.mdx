**A mobile diesel service business sells two things: the repair, and the time the customer does not lose bringing equipment to you.** Most of what decides whether it works is settled before the first job: which work you take, who you take it for, how you price your time, and how you show the customer what you did. This page walks through those decisions in the order they usually come up.

## 1. Decide what work you will take

Mobile work rewards a clear scope. Write down what you will do in the field and what you will send to someone with a bay and a lift.

- **Preventive maintenance.** Scheduled PM is the steadiest mobile work: it is planned, it repeats, and it can be done where the equipment sits. See [what a PM inspection is](/kb/what-is-a-pm-inspection).
- **Road calls and field repairs.** Higher value per call, less predictable, and harder on the schedule.
- **Equipment types.** Trucks and trailers, diesel generators, marine diesel, construction or farm equipment. Each has its own tools, parts, and customers. Starting narrow and widening later is easier than the reverse.
- **Annual DOT inspections.** These come with their own federal qualification for the inspector. Read [how to become a DOT inspector](/kb/who-can-perform-dot-inspection) before you offer them.

## 2. Decide who your customers are

The customer is whoever is responsible for the equipment: an owner-operator, a fleet manager, a maintenance department, an equipment rental company, a property manager with a standby generator, a marina. Each buys differently.

- **Owner-operators** usually buy on trust and response time, one truck at a time.
- **Fleets and maintenance departments** buy on reliability, documentation, and invoicing that fits their process. They are where recurring PM work lives. See [winning fleet PM contracts](/kb/winning-fleet-pm-contracts).
- **Generator and marine customers** often need scheduled service on equipment that sits idle most of the year, which makes a written service history especially valuable to them.

## 3. The truck, the tools, and the parts

Your service vehicle is where the work happens. Plan it around the work you chose in step 1: the tools and diagnostic equipment that work needs, fluid storage and handling, lighting for work on site, and how you will carry or source parts. Know where you will dispose of used oil, filters, and coolant before your first PM.

## 4. Register the business and insure it

Requirements differ by State, county, and city, so this is a checklist of questions to answer, not the answers:

- **Business registration.** How you will register the business with your State, and whether you need a local business license where you are based and where you work.
- **Tax.** Whether you need a federal employer identification number, and how sales tax applies to the parts and labor you sell in your State.
- **Insurance.** Talk to a licensed insurance agent about coverage for your liability on the job, your service vehicle, and customer equipment in your care. Ask specifically about work performed at the customer's location.
- **Environmental rules.** How used fluids and filters must be stored, transported, and disposed of where you work.

None of this is legal, tax, or insurance advice. Confirm the current requirements with your State and local agencies and a licensed professional.

## 5. Price your time before you quote a job

A mobile technician's inventory is time, and the clock starts when you leave for the job, not when the wrench turns. Build your rates from what an hour of your business actually costs, including travel and the hours you cannot bill. See [how to price PM service](/kb/how-to-price-pm-service).

## 6. Decide how the customer will see your work

The customer usually was not standing there when the work was done. What they see is what you send them: what was reported, what you found, what you did, and what comes next. A consistent write-up on every job is how a new business earns the second call.

- Write every job up as [complaint, cause, and correction](/kb/complaint-cause-correction).
- For PM visits, follow the structure in [what belongs in a PM report for the customer](/kb/what-belongs-in-a-pm-report-for-the-customer).
