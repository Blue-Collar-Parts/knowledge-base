**A fleet hands PM work to an outside service provider to keep its equipment running without adding people.** The provider who wins the account is the one who makes the fleet manager's job easier: on time, consistent from unit to unit, and documented well enough that the manager never has to chase a technician to find out what happened.

## What fleet managers look for

- **Reliability.** Showing up when scheduled, and finishing the units planned for the visit.
- **Response time** when something breaks between PMs.
- **Consistency.** The same scope done the same way on every unit, by whoever does the work.
- **Documentation.** A record per unit, per visit, that their team can read and file: what was checked, what was found, what was done, and what needs scheduling next.
- **Invoicing that fits their process,** with unit numbers and work orders that match their records.

## What a fleet maintenance agreement usually defines

Put these in writing before the first visit:

- **The units covered,** identified by unit number and VIN or serial number.
- **The scope of each PM,** and what is excluded.
- **The service interval and how it is set.** The interval for each unit comes from the equipment manufacturer's documentation and the fleet's operating conditions; the agreement says who tracks it.
- **Pricing:** per unit or per hour, trip charges, parts and fluids, and how additional work found during a PM is approved.
- **Authorization:** who on the fleet's side can approve work, and the dollar limit above which you must call first.
- **Response times** for breakdowns, if you offer them.
- **What the fleet receives after each visit,** and in what form.

## How to win the first account

- **Start with the units nobody wants.** Spread-out locations, odd hours, or equipment the fleet's own team does not service. Mobile service providers often get in the door where a fixed location cannot.
- **Offer a defined pilot,** a set number of units for a set period, so the fleet can judge you on results.
- **Show a sample of your write-up.** A fleet manager can judge your documentation before they judge your work. Use the [complaint, cause, correction](/kb/complaint-cause-correction) format and a clean [PM report](/kb/what-belongs-in-a-pm-report-for-the-customer).
- **Price so you can keep the promise.** An account you cannot service on time costs more than it pays. See [how to price PM service](/kb/how-to-price-pm-service).

## How to keep it

Do the same scope the same way every time, report every finding, and send the record for every unit after every visit. When a fleet manager can answer "what was done to unit 214, and when?" without picking up the phone, the account is yours to lose.
